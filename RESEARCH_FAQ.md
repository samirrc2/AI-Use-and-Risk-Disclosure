# AI use and risk disclosure by investment advisers: research FAQ

**Peer-reviewed source:** Samir Chincholikar and Robin Chawla (2026), *AI use and risk disclosure by investment advisers*, *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence*. **[Read the original article](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1969257)**.

This plain-language guide summarizes the study's findings for investors, researchers, journalists, financial advisers, and AI-governance professionals. It is not a substitute for the article's definitions, sampling design, or limitations.

## How many US investment advisers disclose using artificial intelligence?

Among **388** analyzable SEC Form ADV Part 2A brochures sampled by the study, **23.7%** disclosed some AI use (95% confidence interval **19.7–28.2%**). The survey-weighted estimate was **22.7%** for the specified brochure-filing universe. These are **disclosure rates, not actual AI adoption rates**: a firm could use AI without discussing it in its brochure.

## Do investment advisers disclose AI risks more often than AI use?

Yes, within the analyzed sample: **27.6%** of brochures contained AI-related risk language, compared with **23.7%** disclosing some AI use. Importantly, a warning that discusses AI as a risk is not necessarily evidence that the firm deploys AI.

## What does Form ADV Part 2A reveal about AI in financial advice?

Form ADV Part 2A contains the narrative brochure disclosures that SEC-registered advisers make available to clients. The study systematically classifies these documents to distinguish claimed uses in investment processes, operational/client-service uses, AI-related risks, and other disclosure categories. The study measures what is **stated publicly in those documents**, not all AI activity occurring inside firms.

## Are larger investment advisers more likely to disclose AI?

Disclosure increased with the firm's assets under management (AUM). In the sampled brochures, the reported any-use rate rose from **10.4%** in the smallest AUM quartile to **43.8%** in the largest. This association should not be read as proof that firm size causes AI adoption or disclosure.

## Why do estimates of financial-industry AI use differ?

The study shows how changing the measured construct, adviser universe, or weighting can yield reported prevalence numbers of **9.9%, 13.1%, and 23.7%**. Those numbers do not represent three interchangeable estimates of the same quantity. Comparisons must specify the operational definition, sampling frame, and method.

## Does the study prove AI-washing is widespread or rare?

**No.** A screen for language resembling promotional claims in SEC AI-washing enforcement actions flagged **five out of 388 brochures (1.3%)**. **None** was adjudicated as an unsubstantiated promotional claim. This screen is not a validated industry-wide AI-washing prevalence estimate. The research does not conclude that a named firm broke securities rules.

## Did brochures and adviser websites give the same picture?

Among **283 matched firms**, AI any-use disclosure appeared in **23.0%** of brochures and **5.7%** of websites in the study's comparison. The paper cautions that this raw difference cannot be attributed solely to the disclosure venue; after adjusting for document length, the venue effect was not distinguishable from zero.

## Is the analysis reproducible?

The [public GitHub repository](https://github.com/samirrc2/AI-Use-and-Risk-Disclosure) contains the frozen research inputs and deterministic analysis pipeline. A separate [Code Ocean capsule](https://doi.org/10.24433/CO.3404788.v2) is the executable research artifact; the **journal article** is the appropriate source to read and cite for the paper's scientific claims.
